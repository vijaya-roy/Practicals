# Practical 1: Data Privacy Audit of University of Delhi

## Aim

To conduct a data privacy audit of the University of Delhi website and identify how user and applicant data is collected, used, shared, retained and protected.

## Website Selected

**Organization:** University of Delhi

**Website:** University of Delhi — https://www.du.ac.in/

**Privacy Policy:** University of Delhi Generic Privacy Policy and Admission Privacy Policy

## Requirements

- Internet connection
- Web browser
- University of Delhi website
- University of Delhi Privacy Policy

## Procedure

1. Visit the official University of Delhi website.
2. Open and study the Privacy Policy available on the website.
3. Identify the types of information collected from website visitors.
4. Examine the information collected during the admission/application process.
5. Identify the purposes for which the collected information is used.
6. Examine how the information may be shared with University staff and third parties.
7. Check the data retention practices mentioned in the privacy policy.
8. Examine the provisions for correction, deletion and withdrawal of consent.
9. Identify potential privacy risks.
10. Provide recommendations to improve data privacy and transparency.

## Observations

| Privacy Aspect | Observation | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Website Data Collection | The website records information such as server address, top-level domain, browser type, date and time of access, pages accessed, documents downloaded and referring website for statistical purposes. | Medium |
| Email Management | An email address is recorded when a user voluntarily sends a message. It is used only for the purpose for which it was provided and is not added to a mailing list. | Low |
| Personal Information | If additional personal information is requested, users are informed about how it will be used. | Low |
| Admission Data | During the admission process, information such as name, address, phone number, email, date of birth, gender, photograph, educational details, household income and guardian details may be collected. | High |
| Sensitive Data | The admission policy may involve sensitive information such as race/ethnicity, religious beliefs, health or disability information and criminal convictions, where applicable. | High |
| Data Usage | Collected information is used for processing applications, assessing eligibility and other academic and institutional purposes. | Medium |
| Internal Access | Relevant University and college staff may access the information when required, and it may also be shared with University statutory bodies. | Medium |
| Third-Party Sharing | Information may be shared with colleges, educational institutions, government departments, examination bodies, testing services and other specified organizations. | High |
| Data Minimization | The University states that it seeks to share the minimum amount of information necessary with third parties. | Low |
| Data Retention | Information is retained for as long as required for academic, institutional and legal purposes. Retention periods may vary according to the programme and University rules. | Medium |
| Data Correction | Applicants can access, amend or delete their information until the application is submitted. | Low |
| Consent Withdrawal | Where processing is based on consent, the applicant can withdraw consent by contacting the appropriate University authority. | Low |
| External Websites | The University provides links to external websites and portals but does not control the content or reliability of those external pages. | Medium |

## Privacy Risks Identified

1. **Collection of extensive personal data** – The admission process requires a large amount of personal and educational information.
2. **Collection of sensitive information** – Certain applications may involve health, religious, socio-economic and other sensitive information.
3. **Third-party data sharing** – Applicant information may be shared with several external organizations for specified purposes.
4. **Variable data retention** – The retention period may vary depending on the programme and institutional requirements.
5. **External website risks** – Users may access external portals through links from the University website, which are outside the University's direct control.

## Recommendations

1. Collect only the personal information that is necessary for a specific purpose.
2. Apply strong security measures to protect sensitive applicant information.
3. Clearly explain which third parties receive personal data and for what purposes.
4. Provide clearer information about specific data retention periods.
5. Improve privacy awareness among students and applicants.
6. Clearly inform users when they are leaving the University website and accessing an external portal.
7. Regularly review and update privacy policies to reflect current data-processing practices.

## Result

The Data Privacy Audit of the University of Delhi website was successfully performed. The audit identified the types of information collected from website visitors and applicants, the purposes for which the information is used, internal and third-party data sharing practices, retention practices and potential privacy risks.

## Conclusion

The University of Delhi provides publicly available privacy policies describing the collection and use of website and applicant information. The policies include provisions concerning purpose limitation, access, data sharing, retention, correction and consent withdrawal. However, the large amount of personal and sensitive information involved in the admission process creates significant privacy responsibilities. Clearer information about retention periods, third-party processing and privacy practices across external portals could further improve transparency and user privacy.
